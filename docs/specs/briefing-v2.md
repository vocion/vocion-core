# Briefing v2 — CEO product review (Chris Fitkin, 2026-09-16), verbatim

> This review is the spec implemented by the briefing v2 PR — the typed `BriefingV2` document, its validator, composer and renderers under `packages/core/src/services/briefings/`.

## Core diagnosis

The raw intelligence is good. The briefing UX is not.

Right now this feels like a report generated by the agent system about itself, followed by the actual useful revenue briefing. A user opening this page should understand within 20 to 30 seconds: What changed? What matters? What needs my decision? What should happen today?

Instead, the page starts with several sections that effectively say "nothing happened," then presents 661 things needing attention, then says "On track," then drops an entire second briefing into the first briefing.

### What I would attack first

1. The first 25% of the page is mostly dead weight. "Performance is not measured yet," "No runs in this window," "Nothing ran, so nothing to judge yet," and "The next report lands in 24 hours" are system-state messages, not briefing content. If there is no meaningful content for a section, remove the section. Don't make the user read the empty state every morning.

2. "What needs me — 661" destroys the promise of the product. A managed AI workforce is supposed to reduce cognitive load. Showing "657 proposed actions awaiting approval" makes the human feel like they have acquired 657 direct reports. The brief should say something more like: "3 decisions need you today. 658 lower-priority items are queued." Then show the three. I would strongly distinguish: Decisions requiring judgment, approvals safe to batch, and background queue. Never make the raw queue count the headline.

3. "Are we on track — On track" is not credible. The very next sentence is "Nothing ran, so nothing to judge yet." Those statements conflict. If you cannot judge performance, don't give a green status. Use "Not enough evidence", or better, omit the section until the user has defined an outcome and target.

4. You have a briefing inside a briefing. "From the revops briefing" starts another dated briefing, another generated timestamp, then a long report with its own sections and hierarchy. This exposes the agent/org structure instead of synthesizing it. The workspace briefing should be the editorial layer above the team briefings. It should ingest RevOps, Deal Desk, Founder GTM, etc., resolve duplication, prioritize across them, and produce one coherent answer. "From the RevOps briefing" should probably disappear entirely.

5. The page reports state when it should report change. "$3.52M open pipeline" is useful once. Tomorrow morning I mostly care whether it became $3.72M, whether $300K slipped, whether something entered contract, or whether forecast confidence deteriorated. The current briefing gives a detailed snapshot but very little explicit delta. Every recurring briefing should be built around "since the last brief":
   Open pipeline $3.52M ↑ $210K · Contracts out $846K no change · 2 opportunities advanced · 1 close date slipped · 3 call outcomes missing

6. Your best content is buried near the bottom. "The 3 Moves That Matter Most" is exactly what I want a briefing to give me, yet it comes after pipeline, calls, contracts, hot deals, MQLs, CRM gaps, stale deals, zombie pipeline, and yesterday's changes. That section belongs at the top, immediately after a short state-of-the-business summary.

7. Implementation details are leaking into the executive UX. "`hs_deal_stage_probability` exists in HubSpot's schema but is not returned by the counting tools available" is good transparency for debugging, but it does not belong in the primary briefing. Render this as something like "Weighted forecast unavailable · Why?" with the technical detail behind a disclosure.

8. The agent roster is the wrong abstraction for this page. The Deal Desk, Founder GTM, RevOps, and Unassigned tables are all zeroes. The briefing should not make me inspect whether `proposal-writer` ran 0/0 times. That belongs in Team Report or observability. On a briefing, surface agent operations only when something matters: failures, unusual spend, stalled work, missed SLA, human intervention, or a major completed outcome.

9. "Evidence" is system telemetry, not evidence. `Runs 0 · Completed 0 · Failed 0 · Spend $0 · Tokens 0...` doesn't substantiate the business claims above it. I'd rename this to "Run details" or "System details", collapse it by default, and make actual evidence attach to individual claims: HubSpot record, email, call transcript, DocuSeal contract, etc.

10. History turns the page into an archive. The previous briefs list runs for dozens of entries and consumes a huge portion of the page. Show the last 3 to 5. Then "View all briefings". Search/filter can live on the archive view.

## The information architecture I would ship

| Order | Section | What it answers | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today | What is the state of the business right now? | 3 to 5 metrics with deltas, one sentence |
| 2 | Needs your decision | What can only the human decide? | Maximum 3 to 5 action cards |
| 3 | What changed | What is materially different since the previous brief? | Delta-driven, not snapshot-driven |
| 4 | Today's critical path | What meetings, signatures, deadlines, or follow-ups matter today? | Time/order based |
| 5 | Risks & exceptions | What is off-plan, contradictory, stalled, or missing? | Only actual exceptions |
| 6 | Pipeline / functional detail | What do I inspect if I want more depth? | Compact table, expandable |
| 7 | Agent activity | Did my AI workforce operate correctly? | One-line summary, expandable |
| 8 | Sources & run details | Where did this come from? Can I trust it? | Collapsed provenance |
| 9 | Previous briefings | What was said before? | Last 3 to 5 + View all |

Key principle: Organize the briefing around the human's decisions and outcomes, not around the agent hierarchy that produced it.

## Content rules to bake into Vocion itself

- A briefing has a hard attention budget: ~5 major items above the fold; no more than 3 items that "need you" unless there is a genuine incident.
- Every item answers why this matters now. "$450K contract unsigned" is data. "$450K contract unsigned while delivery has already started" is a briefing.
- Every recurring metric prefers a delta over an absolute number.
- Every recommendation exposes the evidence behind it.
- Every action has a named owner or a direct action.
- Internal system architecture (agent names, job names, tokens, raw runs, connector limitations) stays behind progressive disclosure unless it materially affects the outcome.

The page today proves Vocion can gather and reason over a lot of data. The next step is making it prove that Vocion knows what not to show you. That is the bigger product signal for an AI workforce.
