# Internal linking

Internal links tell Google which pages matter and how they relate, and tell a
reader where to go next. On a service business's site they have one job above
the others: move a reader from a question to the service page that answers it
with a booking.

## Hubs and the pages under them

- Each service has a hub: its service page. Blog posts and location pages
  about that service link up to it; the hub links down to the best of them.
- A service-areas hub links every location page; each location page links
  back up, out to the services that matter there, and across to one or two
  neighbouring areas.
- Breadcrumbs follow the same hierarchy, and important pages sit within three
  clicks of the home page.

## Every new page, both directions

- **Out:** 3 to 6 contextual links in the body, from the client's site
  inventory. Every target is a live URL (status 200), never a redirect or a
  404.
- **In:** at least two existing, relevant pages link to the new one once it is
  live. The brief names them; publishing adds them or lists them as the
  follow-up. A page with no internal link pointing at it is an orphan: Google
  finds it late and ranks it low.

## Anchors

- The anchor says what the target is: "water heater repair in Fairview",
  "what a tankless conversion costs". Never "click here", "learn more" or a
  bare URL.
- Vary anchors naturally, but never use the same anchor for two different
  targets: that tells Google two pages answer one query.
- Link a target once per page. Later links to the same URL add nothing.
- Body links beat navigation and footer links. Never a block of keyword links
  at the bottom of a page.

## Links to the service pages

Every blog post links to the service page it supports, in the paragraph where
the reader realises they need the service, not only in a closing line. Every
location page links to the services it describes. This is the link we check
first.

## Cannibalisation

When two pages compete for the same query, we do not write a third. Link the
weaker page to the stronger with the query as the anchor, and flag the pair to
the strategist to consolidate (one keyword, one page; `seo_plan_batch` refuses
a keyword the project already has unless it is a refresh).
