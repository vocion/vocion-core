# Sourcing and claims

A page that states one invented number cannot be trusted on anything else it
says, by a reader, by the client or by Google's quality raters. The rule is
simple: every claim can be traced in one click, or it is not on the page.

## Every number has a source and a date

- Link each figure inline to the primary source: a government agency, a
  standards body, the manufacturer's spec sheet, the utility, a peer-reviewed
  study. A blog quoting a statistic is not the source; find the original or
  cut the number.
- Anything that changes over time carries its date in the sentence: "as of
  March 2026", "the 2025 code cycle", "the rebate the utility lists for 2026".
- Prices come from the client's own price list, with its date, or from a
  dated and linked source. A range averaged from competitors' pages is not a
  price.
- When no source can be found, write the point without the number ("most
  tank heaters last a decade or more" becomes a sourced figure or goes), and
  list the cut in your reply.

## Never

- "Studies show", "experts agree", "up to 50%" or any figure with no link.
- A statistic, survey or percentage produced by a model.
- A rounded-up or combined claim ("hundreds of five-star reviews" when the
  profile shows 140 reviews averaging 4.8).
- A claim about a competitor that is not in the SERP brief, dated. We never
  disparage a competitor by name.

## Client proof, only as worded

- Testimonials are quoted exactly as the customer wrote them, from the review
  platform or a source on the project, with the name as the review shows it.
  No trimming that changes the meaning, no stitching two reviews together.
- Results are stated the way the client stated them, citing where ("cut
  callbacks by a third", only if the project's source says so).
- Awards, certifications and memberships name the issuing body and the year.
  Licence numbers appear when the client lists them.
- The project's never-claim list is checked in code; a draft with one goes
  back whatever the editor says.

## Safety, money and law

For gas, electrical, structural, health, legal or financial topics, stay
inside what the client is licensed to do and say. A gas smell is "leave the
building and call the gas utility's emergency line", never steps to find the
leak. Code requirements cite the code and its edition, and say the local
authority decides.

## E-E-A-T: show who is speaking and what they have done

- Every page is signed by a real person from the project's authors, linked to
  their author page, with the credentials that matter for this topic. Never a
  made-up persona, never a generated headshot.
- When the author is not the practitioner, a "Reviewed by" line names the real
  licensed person who checked it, and only if they did.
- Show first-hand experience: what the crew finds on jobs, the client's own
  photos, steps only someone who does the work would know, the mistakes they
  see homeowners make. This is the part no other page has.
- `datePublished` and `dateModified` are honest. A date moves only when the
  content changed in substance.

## How it is checked

The writer links sources inline and lists every claim it cut. The editor
quotes any untraced number, claim or testimonial as a `fix`, with the line it
is on.
