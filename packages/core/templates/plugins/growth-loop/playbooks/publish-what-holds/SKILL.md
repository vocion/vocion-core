# Publish what holds

Anything published under the workspace's name cannot be unsaid. The class of the
claim decides how much has to be behind it and how much of a person's attention
it costs before it goes out. The classes are ordered by what a retraction costs,
not by how often the claim is made.

> **This file is a stub on purpose.** The five classes and the shape below hold
> for anybody publishing anything. The thresholds, the named authority for each
> class and the regulated categories are a workspace's own — replace this file
> whole in `playbooks/publish-what-holds/SKILL.md` with yours.

## `descriptive` — it says what the thing is

What it needs: the description matches the thing as it exists today, and
`verifiedAgainst` says which version that was.

The common failure is not exaggeration, it is **staleness** — a true sentence
about a previous release. Anything describing a moving thing carries its pin.

## `instructional` — it tells somebody how to do something

What it needs: somebody has done it, in that order, and it worked. An
instruction nobody has followed end to end is a guess with numbered steps.

Where a step involves something irreversible, the piece says so before the step,
not after.

## `comparative` — it says how the thing stands against a named alternative

What it needs: the comparison verified this quarter, against the alternative as
it is configured today, with the date it was checked and the configuration it
was checked in. A comparison drawn from somebody else's marketing page is not a
comparison.

Never compare against a named alternative on a dimension nobody measured. "Ours
is simpler" is an opinion; say it as one or drop it.

This class always takes a person's decision. A retraction here is public and it
is about somebody else.

## `performance` — it puts a number on an outcome

What it needs: the measurement, the conditions it was taken under, the sample
size, and the date. A figure without all four is unsupported, however true it
happens to be.

Numbers from a test environment are never published as numbers from a real one.
A range beats a single figure whenever the underlying readings vary.

## `regulated` — a claim whose wording is governed by somebody outside

What it needs: whatever that authority says, and a person who is accountable for
having checked. This class never earns its way past a person on any ledger,
however long the record of good decisions. A workspace names its categories and
its authority here.

## The rule that spans all five

A claim the workspace cannot defend in one move — pointing at the evidence,
today, without a conversation — does not go out. "I could not establish this"
sends a piece back; it never becomes a hedge inside one.
