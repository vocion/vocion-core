# Measure before you make

Chosen afterwards, any piece looks like a success under some number. Chosen
first, it can fail honestly — and honest failure is the only thing the loop
learns from.

## Four things, fixed in advance

1. **The number**, in the workspace's own vocabulary.
2. **The baseline** — where it stood when the brief was written. Taken before,
   or the brief is not ready. A baseline reconstructed afterwards is a baseline
   chosen to suit the result.
3. **The source** — the system the reading comes from, named so anybody can take
   it again. A measure with no source is not a measure.
4. **The attribution model** — first touch, last touch, multi-touch, holdout,
   none. Identical data produces different winners under different models, and a
   reading that does not say which cannot be argued with.

And the **date the reading is due**, so a piece measured after three days is
never silently compared with one measured after ninety.

## "We cannot measure this" is a real answer

Where no source exists, or the volume is too small for any reading to mean
anything, say so at brief time and expect the verdict `unmeasurable`. Name the
instrument that is missing and what it would cost to have it.

What not to do: substitute a proxy nobody agreed to because it happens to be
available. A number that can be read but does not answer the question is worse
than a gap, because it will be reported, argued over and acted on.

## Never a rate on four events

Volume decides whether a ratio means anything. Below the threshold where it
does, report the counts. A conversion rate on four events is a story about four
people wearing the clothes of a statistic.

## Establish normal variation before calling a change

Know what the number does when nothing happens. A movement inside that range is
`no_effect`, and reporting it as a win is how a team spends a year repeating
something that never worked.

## Unmeasured is reported, never omitted

A channel or a piece with no reading is listed as unread, with the reason. An
omitted one reads as one with no return, which is a claim nobody made and
nobody checked.
