# Extending the team, within a budget

A team that can add to itself is a team that can run away. Two things stop that,
and neither of them is the prompt: **the hire is a proposal a person decides and
can undo**, and **it carries the allowance it is hired under**.

## Name the gap, then the role

The evidence for a hire is work that waited, not a feeling about capacity.

> Four briefs in the last two weeks needed somebody to decide which page owns
> which question. Three of them were produced without that decision and two came
> back from the gate for competing with an existing piece. Nobody owns it.

That is a gap. "We should have a specialist" is not, and neither is "the backlog
is growing" — a growing backlog is usually a ranking problem, and adding a
teammate to an unranked backlog produces more unranked work, faster.

Two weeks with no owner is the bar. Below that, the answer is ranking, a clearer
brief, or dropping the work.

## Hire from the catalog, do not invent a role

The proposal names a **catalog slug**. The definition being installed was
authored and reviewed by whoever ships the catalog, which is what makes this
safe to put on the ladder at all: the team picks a teammate, it does not write
one. If no catalog role fits the gap, that is a finding for a person — say what
the missing role would do, and stop.

## The allowance is part of the hire

`dailyCentsLimit` is required. It becomes the new teammate's soft and hard daily
cap the moment the hire lands, so the first day cannot run away while nobody is
looking. A workspace loosens it afterwards on the budgets page, deliberately,
rather than discovering the number later.

Pick it from what the work costs, not from what feels generous: what the briefs
that waited would have cost to produce, plus a margin. Say where the number came
from in the reason.

## Read the headroom first

Before proposing anything, read what the workspace has committed and spent
today. If the allowance you want is more than what is left, ask for less or say
the allowance has to rise first. Proposing a hire the gate will refuse wastes a
person's attention and teaches them to distrust the next one.

The gate refuses two cases outright, so a card only ever reaches a person when
the money is actually there:

- the workspace has already spent its committed daily allowance;
- the allowance asked for is larger than what is unspent.

A workspace that has set no budgets at all has no ceiling, and the card says so
plainly rather than implying one.

## What the card says, and what undo takes back

The card names the role, its team, the skills it composes, what it reaches for
when connected, the daily allowance, and the workspace's headroom beside it.
Approving creates the agent from the catalog definition, opens its team if the
workspace has none, and caps it. **Undo removes all three** — the agent, the
budget row, and the team if the hire created it.

Hiring is not on the learning-eagerness dial. That dial is for the system
changing what it *knows*; this changes what it can *do*, and it keeps a bar of
its own.

## When the answer is no

The gap stays open and you say what it is costing — the briefs still waiting,
the rework at the gate — every week, until somebody closes it another way. A
rejected hire is not a closed question; it is a decision that this gap is
cheaper than this teammate, and that trade is worth re-checking when the cost
changes.

Never propose the same role again inside a month on the same evidence. New
evidence, or wait.
