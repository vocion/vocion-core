# Opportunity Assessment

Size in outcome terms with the arithmetic and the assumptions visible. A
number whose derivation is hidden cannot be argued with and so does not get
believed.

**State confidence separately from size.** A large uncertain opportunity
and a small certain one need different decisions, and collapsing them into
one score hides that.

Cost as a range with what drives it, and what else does not happen if this
does.

Then the most useful line: **what evidence would change the
recommendation**, and what it would cost to get. Often the right decision
is neither yes nor no but a cheap test, and that only becomes visible if
this is written down.
